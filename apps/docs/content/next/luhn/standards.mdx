# Standards and modulo bias

`@evanion/luhn` over the dictionary `'0123456789'` is Luhn mod-10, the
standardised check digit on credit card numbers. Over any other dictionary it
is Luhn mod-N, which no standard defines.

## Luhn mod-10 matches the published standard

`createLuhn({ dictionary: '0123456789' })` reproduces the published mod-10 test
vectors, inputs with known check digits. Baize's order number written as the
digits `20260042` gets the check digit `5`:

```ts twoslash file=libs/luhn/README.md region=mod-10

```

Luhn's patent [US 2,950,048](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2950048A/en)
defines the algorithm. ISO/IEC 7812-1 Annex B specifies it for issuer
identification numbers on payment cards. The mobile-network standards body 3GPP
restates it in TS 23.003 Annex B.2, and the US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid
Services (CMS) restate it in the
[National Provider Identifier check-digit specification](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Administrative-Simplification/NationalProvIdentStand/Downloads/NPIcheckdigit.pdf).

## Luhn mod-N has no standard

No RFC, ISO, ITU or ANSI document defines the Luhn sum `@evanion/luhn` computes
over an arbitrary dictionary. The usual formula for a
doubled character, `floor(2x / n) + (2x mod n)` for index `x` in a dictionary of `n`
characters, traces to
[Wikipedia revision 74161899](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Luhn_mod_N_algorithm&oldid=74161899),
dated 2006-09-06, which was unsourced then and is unsourced now. Several
implementations say in their own comments that they were transliterated from
that page. With any `n` other than 10, `@evanion/luhn`
implements that common generalisation and claims nothing more.

- Do not assume another mod-N implementation agrees. Check it against test
  vectors, inputs with known check characters, and not against the name.
- The dictionary's order is part of the algorithm. Two libraries agreeing on the
  formula and disagreeing on the alphabet produce different check characters.

The formula is Luhn's original formulation. The patent describes "twice the
original digit plus an end around carry … the addition of any digit standing in
the tens position to the digit standing in the units position", which is adding
the two base-`n` digits of a doubled index. Decimal Luhn is often taught as
"subtract 9 from a doubled digit above 9", which gives the same sum when `n` is 10.

## The one adjacent swap a Luhn check character misses

`@evanion/luhn` adds a doubled index `x` as `g(x) = floor(2x / n) + (2x mod n)`,
the formula
[What `createLuhn` checks](../dictionaries#what-createluhn-checks-in-a-dictionary)
uses to show why `n` is even, and an undoubled index as `x`. Take two adjacent characters with index
`a` at the undoubled position and index `b` at the doubled one. Swapping them
goes unnoticed exactly when `a + g(b) ≡ b + g(a) (mod n)`. `createLuhn` accepts
only an even `n`, and for an even `n` the only pair satisfying that is
`{0, n - 1}`, the dictionary's first and last characters: `0` and `z` in the
default, and `0` and `9` in mod-10.

## Modulo bias over a luhn dictionary

Drawing a random code over a luhn dictionary with `byte % n` picks every
character equally often only when `n` divides 256. The default dictionary does
not: `256 % 36` is 4, so `0` to `3` come up about 14% more often than the other
32 characters. `uniformOverBytes` reports whether an instance's dictionary is
safe to draw from that way:

```ts twoslash file=libs/luhn/README.md region=modulo-bias

```

`@evanion/luhn` does not generate random values. `uniformOverBytes` says
nothing about the quality of your bytes, and `crypto.getRandomValues` is the
source to draw them from.

A dictionary of 32 characters divides 256 and leaves room to drop the four
confusable characters `i`, `l`, `o` and `w` from 36. Rejection sampling is the
alternative: draw a byte, discard it when it is one of the top `256 % n`
values, 252 to 255 for the default, and draw again. It is correct and it makes
generation take a variable number of draws, which is why `@evanion/token` constrains its alphabet to 32 instead.
