# Encoding and comparing URNs

`encodeNss` in `@evanion/urn` turns a title a person typed, such as
`Brass: Birmingham`, into an NSS, and `equals` compares two URNs the way RFC
8141 §3.1 does.

## Put a game title into a URN

`encodeNss` in `@evanion/urn` leaves letters, digits and `-._~` alone and
percent-encodes every other character, writing each of its UTF-8 bytes as a `%`
and two hex digits, so `é` becomes `%C3%A9`.
`decodeNss` reverses it. `stringify` and `parse` do neither:

```ts twoslash file=libs/urn/README.md region=nss-encoding

```

`encodeNss` also encodes the `:`, which the NSS grammar allows unencoded, so
two services that both encode a title write the same NSS. `equals` treats `%3A`
and `:` as different characters, so `Brass%3A%20Birmingham`, encoded in full,
and `Brass:%20Birmingham`, with only its space encoded, name two different
games.

## Compare URNs from two services

`equals` in `@evanion/urn` applies RFC 8141 §3.1. It ignores case in the
scheme, in the NID and in the hex digits of a percent-encoded character, and
compares the rest of the NSS character for character:

The third call returns `false` because `equals` never decodes `%3A`. The fourth
returns `false` because the NSS keeps its case, so `Azul` and `azul` are two
games. Call `equals` wherever URNs arrive from more than one service.

## Carry a URN in a URL path

`encodeURIComponent` makes a whole `@evanion/urn` URN safe as one segment of a
URL path, where `:` and `%` mean something to a router. `encodeNss` makes a
string safe as an NSS. A URN built from a title needs both, NSS first:

```ts twoslash file=libs/urn/README.md region=url-segment

```

The `%3A` inside the NSS becomes `%253A` in the path, and one
`decodeURIComponent` turns it back. A second decode would turn `%3A` into `:`
and `%20` into a space, and `parse` throws on the space.
