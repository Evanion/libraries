`@evanion/acl` decides from two inputs: a frozen JSON document, and the subject
and object bags a caller already holds. Four questions sit outside those inputs,
and each one is a property of the model rather than a feature nobody has written
yet. A team that needs one of the four is better served by another tool, and the
section covering it names which.

## Revocation reaches a consumer no sooner than `maxStale`

`expiryOf` (`libs/acl/src/hydrate-policy.ts:188-205`) settles the instant past
which every key answers `stale-contract`, at
`fetchedAt + min(matrix.maxStale, options.maxStale)`. A consumer holding a
document fetched before the owner edited it goes on deciding from the old rules
until it revalidates. `maxStale` is therefore the floor on revocation latency,
and no export of this library lowers it.

Executed against `libs/acl/src` with `maxStale: 1000`, `fetchedAt: 0` and a
settled clock of 5000:

- a key the document carries answers `stale-contract`
- a key the document does not carry answers `stale-contract`
- `capabilities` answers `stale-contract` for every entry
- `readsObject` answers `true`, because it states a fact about the document and
  none about the present

An owner who needs a revocation to take effect inside one second states
`maxStale: 1000`, and every consumer revalidates at that rate. Two enterprise
cases separate at this point, and a deployment that confuses them sets the
budget far lower than it needs. A terminated employee is revoked at the identity
provider, which reaches the subject: `subject.roles` stops carrying the role and
every consumer resolving a fresh subject refuses at once, whatever the
document's age. Revocation through the document is for a change of rule. An
emergency grant written into the document carries the same bound in the worse
direction, so a break-glass design puts the emergency role on the subject and
the condition in the document, and the document names the role it honours and
never the person.

## A relationship across two rows has no expression in a matrix

"Is this question in a shop this bookseller's team owns" states a hop, and a
matrix condition reads one field of one scope. `readPath`
(`libs/acl/src/conditions.ts:76-92`) splits a path at its first dot and reads
the remainder as a single own key off the `subject` or `object` bag.
`pathFault` (`libs/acl/src/validate.ts:57-72`) refuses the deeper form at
construction, so a policy naming one throws before it decides anything:

```text no-run
InvalidConditionError: permission "question.edit": condition rules[0].when[0] on "object.shop.ownerId" nests below its scope: a path reads one field of a scope, so at most one dot is resolvable
```

`ObjectSchema.relations` (`libs/acl/src/types.ts:176-183`) declares which kinds
one kind points at, one hop and no nesting. A condition cannot compare a
relation, because a relation is no value. The field is declared for the
consumers that resolve the hop themselves.

Traversal needs the graph, a matrix carries no graph, and fetching one during a
decision is the network call this design exists to avoid. A caller that has
already resolved the hop passes the result as a flat field, and
`object.teamId` on a loaded row is an ordinary condition that decides
correctly. An answer that depends on walking a hierarchy the caller does not
hold belongs to a Zanzibar implementation, where a relationship tuple is the
primitive and the graph walk is the engine's job:
[SpiceDB](https://authzed.com/docs/spicedb/getting-started/discovering-spicedb)
and [OpenFGA](https://openfga.dev/docs/getting-started/setup-openfga/docker).
Each runs as a service over a database, and each check is a network call.

## No call answers which subjects can take an action

An access review asks who can approve a refund. A matrix answers that a subject
whose `roles` contains `approver` and whose `tenantId` equals the order's can,
and converting that second answer into the first needs the subject population. A
matrix carries no subject population, and no entry point of `@evanion/acl` ever
receives one.

Attribute-based access control (ABAC) carries this limit generally, so it
belongs to the model and not to this implementation. NIST SP 800-162 §3.1.2.3
calls the review of who holds access before any request is made a
before-the-fact audit, often necessary for demonstrating compliance, and states
that "An ABAC system may not lend itself well to conducting these audits
efficiently". The same page records that determining who can reach an object may
require simulating the request for every known subject. The join belongs to the
identity directory, and what a matrix contributes to it is a machine-readable
statement of the conditions per permission.

The inverse question is answerable and is the stronger evidence. What gated
`order.refund` on 3 March is the document at the version deployed on 3 March.

## Every consumer keeps its own decision records

Evaluation is a local call inside each consumer process, so N consumers produce
N streams of decision records and no stream holds every decision. A question
nobody asked produces no record at all, which makes an absence of records no
evidence that no access occurred: a caller that skipped the check entirely
leaves nothing behind, and no party here owns the call count that would show the
gap.

NIST SP 800-53 AU-2 asks an organization for "a rationale for why the event
types selected for logging are deemed to be adequate". A deployment on
`@evanion/acl` writes that rationale itself, naming which call sites are
instrumented and why the uninstrumented ones do not matter.

## Which record is the access record

A consumer's decision records are user-interface evidence, and the records of
the service that owns the row are access evidence. Every layer re-evaluates and
no layer's answer travels forward as an assertion, so a browser's `can` answered
what a screen drew and never what a write was permitted to do. An audit that
presents a browser's decision log as an access record is presenting the wrong
artifact. [The security contract](/acl/security) states where a decision binds.

| What a compliance reviewer asks          | What answers it                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| the control's definition on a given date | the matrix at the `version` deployed on that date           |
| evidence that a change was authorised    | the git history: an author, a reviewer, a timestamp, a diff |
| evidence that the control operated       | the decision records of the service that owns the row       |
| a list of who held access                | nothing here; the subject population is the directory's     |

## What will not be built

Nine features are refused for `@evanion/acl`, each because of the property it
would break. The list is fixed rather than a backlog, so a team reading it can
plan around the refusals instead of waiting on them.

| Refused                                                           | The property it breaks                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a remote decision endpoint, a sidecar or a decision-point service | every property of a local decision becomes conditional on the network                             |
| relationship traversal or a graph walk                            | the document carries no graph, and fetching one during a decision reaches the network             |
| a fetcher, a poller or a loader taking a URL                      | a caller constructing inside a request handler puts a network call on the decision path           |
| a push revocation channel                                         | a consumer that missed a push believes it is fresh, and `maxStale` still bounds the window anyway |
| a decision cache                                                  | a cache adds a key to get wrong and a second staleness window inside the process                  |
| a decision sink, a log transport or an audit shipper              | the application's logging stack already holds retention, redaction and a destination              |
| a reverse index over subjects                                     | it needs the subject population, so the library would hold a copy of the identity directory       |
| an administration interface that writes the matrix                | the document would change outside review, and review is the only control over an over-broad grant |
| a per-consumer freshness override that extends the owner's budget | `expiryOf` takes `min` of the two, because a consumer choosing its own bound optimises for itself |

Two of the nine get asked for most: relationship traversal and the reverse index
over subjects. Both are answered better by naming SpiceDB or OpenFGA than by
half-building either here. Tightening the freshness budget below the owner's
stays allowed, and an interface that opens a pull request against the matrix is
a different thing and nobody's objection.
